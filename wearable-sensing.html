Force–voltage characterization of a wearable triboelectric generator for biomechanical sensing.

Institution Western University
Lab Knee Biomechanics Lab
Year 2025
Role Undergraduate Researcher

01 / The Challenge

Understanding sensor response under biomechanical loading.

Triboelectric generators can convert mechanical loading into electrical signals, creating opportunities for self-powered biomechanical sensing. This project investigated the relationship between applied force and generated voltage under dynamic loading conditions using a wearable experimental platform.

02 / Design & Prototyping

From sensing element to wearable prototype.

I designed and fabricated a wearable prototype incorporating four triboelectric sensing locations paired with load cells. The system enabled simultaneous measurement of applied force and generated voltage at different regions of the foot.

03 / Experimental Testing

Building a repeatable measurement setup.

I developed and used an instrumented experimental setup to acquire force and voltage measurements across four sensing locations. Controlled testing was used to characterize sensor behaviour and compare responses across the wearable system.

04 / Results

Quantifying the relationship between force and voltage.

Peak voltage generally increased with applied force, while the magnitude of the response varied between sensing locations. These differences highlighted the influence of local loading conditions on sensor performance.

05 / Biomedical Significance

Toward self-powered load sensing.

The wearable system demonstrated the feasibility of collecting paired force–voltage measurements under dynamic loading conditions. These data can support development of models that estimate mechanical loading from triboelectric sensor output, contributing toward the longer-term goal of self-powered load sensing for smart orthopedic implants.
