Force–voltage characterization of a wearable triboelectric generator for biomechanical sensing.

Institution Western University
Lab Knee Biomechanics Lab
Year 2025
Role Undergraduate Researcher

01 / The Challenge

Understanding sensor response under biomechanical loading.

Triboelectric generators convert mechanical loading into electrical signals, creating opportunities for self-powered biomechanical sensing. This project investigated the relationship between applied force and generated voltage under dynamic loading conditions using a wearable experimental platform.

02 / Design & Prototyping

From sensing element to wearable prototype.

I designed and fabricated a wearable prototype incorporating four triboelectric sensing locations paired with load cells, enabling simultaneous measurement of applied force and generated voltage at different regions of the foot.

Fabricated triboelectric sensing elements.

CAD development of the sensing-element housing.

Four sensing locations distributed across the wearable sole.

03 / Experimental Testing

Instrumenting the system for simultaneous measurement.

An instrumented experimental setup was used to acquire force and voltage measurements across the four sensing locations. Controlled testing enabled characterization of sensor behaviour and comparison of responses across the wearable system.

04 / Results

Characterizing force–voltage response.

Simultaneous force and voltage measurements demonstrated distinct sensor responses across the four sensing locations.

~9 V ~100 N · Channel A
~5 V ~100 N · Channel B
~15 V ~50 N · Channel C
~20 V ~100 N · Channel D

Peak voltage increased with applied force, while differences between sensing locations highlighted the influence of local loading conditions on triboelectric sensor performance.

05 / Biomedical Significance

Toward self-powered load sensing.

The system demonstrated the feasibility of collecting paired force–voltage measurements under dynamic loading conditions. These data can support development of models that estimate mechanical loading from triboelectric sensor output, contributing toward the longer-term goal of self-powered load sensing for smart orthopedic implants.
